Lesson 05 of 13

Thread Your Machine

Follow the upper thread path, seat the thread inside the tension system, and prepare your machine for a successful test stitch.

Time
10–15 minutes
Level
Complete beginner
You’ll need
Machine, thread, scissors, scrap fabric
Having issues?
Step 1 of 8
01

BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Prepare your machine

Turn the machine off. Raise the presser foot, then turn the handwheel toward you until the needle and thread take-up lever are at their highest positions.

  • Machine is turned off
  • Presser foot is raised
  • Needle is fully raised
  • Take-up lever is visible
Sage’s Tip

Raising the presser foot opens the tension discs. If you thread with the foot lowered, the thread can miss the tension system even when the path looks correct.

↑Raise before threading

Presser foot, needle, and take-up lever

02

POSITION THE THREAD

Place the spool on the spool pin

Place your spool on the vertical or horizontal spool pin. If your machine uses a spool cap, choose one that holds the spool securely without preventing it from turning.

Common mistake

Do not press the spool cap tightly against the thread. Leave enough room for the spool to unwind smoothly.

Let the spool turn freely

Follow the direction shown in your manual

03

FOLLOW THE PATH

Use the upper thread guide

Pull the thread from the spool and pass it through or underneath the first guide on top of your machine. Follow the solid line, arrows, or numbers printed on your machine.

Hold the thread lightly near the spool with one hand while guiding it with the other. This slight tension helps it settle into each guide.

→Spool to first guide

Do not follow the separate bobbin-winding path

04

ENGAGE THE TENSION

Guide the thread down and back up

Draw the thread down the first channel, around the bottom, and back up through the second channel. The motion usually forms a long U shape.

Check it

With the presser foot raised, the thread should move easily. After threading, lowering the foot should create noticeably more resistance.

UDown, around, and up

Keep gentle tension on the thread

05

DO NOT SKIP THIS

Thread the take-up lever

At the top of the second channel, guide the thread through the opening in the take-up lever. Most machines thread the lever from right to left. Follow the arrows on your machine.

Pull the thread gently to confirm it remains inside the lever, then bring it back down toward the needle.

Can’t find the lever?

Turn the handwheel toward you until the lever reaches its highest position.

↶Catch the take-up lever

A missed lever often causes tangles underneath

06

CONTROL THE THREAD

Use the lower thread guides

Guide the thread through every remaining guide above the needle. Most machines have a small guide on the needle bar or directly above the needle.

Sage’s Tip

Small guides are easy to miss, but they keep the thread aligned while the needle moves. Do not skip one because it looks unimportant.

↓Follow every guide

Continue toward the needle

07

FINAL THREADING STEP

Thread the needle

Cut the thread end cleanly if it is fuzzy. Pass it through the needle eye in the direction specified by your manual. Many modern machines thread from front to back, but not all do.

Pull 4–6 inches of thread through the needle and place the tail underneath the presser foot toward the back.

Safety reminder

Keep the machine turned off whenever your fingers are near the needle.

Leave a 4–6 inch tail

Place it under the foot toward the back

08

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Make a test stitch

  1. Place two layers of scrap woven fabric under the presser foot.
  2. Hold both thread tails gently behind the machine.
  3. Lower the presser foot and select a straight stitch.
  4. Slowly sew 4–6 stitches.
  5. Check both sides of the fabric.
Your stitches should:
  • Look even on both sides
  • Lie flat without puckering
  • Have no large loops underneath
  • Move smoothly without unusual noises
Something doesn’t look right
✓Ready to sew

The sewing result is your check-in—no quiz needed

Having issues?

Start with what you are seeing.

There are loops underneath my fabric

The upper thread probably missed the tension discs or take-up lever. Raise the presser foot, remove the upper thread completely, and rethread from the beginning.

The upper thread keeps breaking

Check for a snag at the spool, confirm the spool cap is not too tight, rethread the machine, and inspect the needle for damage.

The thread comes out when I begin

Leave a 4–6 inch tail and hold both thread tails gently behind the presser foot for the first few stitches.

The thread feels unusually tight

Stop pulling. Check for a snag around the spool or guide. Raise the presser foot and rethread rather than forcing the thread.
